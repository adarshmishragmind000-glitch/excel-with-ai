What you will learn and use

Tools & working environment

Computer with a modern browser, networking utilities, Linux basics and intentionally vulnerable training labs. Some activities may use screenshots/sample logs.

How the learning works

  • Learn the concept through guided instruction and examples.
  • Complete two practical assignments each week.
  • Submit your work for review and improve it using feedback.
  • Keep your project files and notes organised as a portfolio.

What you should have by the end

  • Track-specific practical files and task submissions.
  • One completed final project with documentation.
  • Evidence of your work such as screenshots, code, workbook or report.
  • Experience explaining your approach in a project demonstration.
  • A clearer view of the next skills to learn for entry-level roles.
WEEK-BY-WEEK PLAN

Your complete 6-week roadmap

Each week includes two practical assignments. Exact task sheets, submission dates, mentor review schedule and delivery mode will be shared during onboarding.

WEEK 01Security foundations

Week 1 — Security foundations

What you will learn

  • Learn confidentiality, integrity, availability, threats, vulnerabilities and risk.
  • Set up a safe learning workspace and understand written authorisation boundaries.

Practical assignments

  • Create a personal device/account security checklist.
  • Review password strength concepts and enable MFA on a test account where possible.

Weekly submission: Complete both practical tasks and submit files/screenshots/notes as instructed.

WEEK 02Networking basics

Week 2 — Networking basics

What you will learn

  • Understand IP, DNS, HTTP/HTTPS, ports and client/server basics.
  • Read basic network configuration and sample traffic in a lab.

Practical assignments

  • Draw a simple home/office network diagram.
  • Interpret supplied sample network output and document observations.

Weekly submission: Complete both practical tasks and submit files/screenshots/notes as instructed.

WEEK 03System and web safety

Week 3 — System and web safety

What you will learn

  • Learn updates, permissions, backups, phishing and secure browsing.
  • Study common web risks using guided, intentionally vulnerable labs only.

Practical assignments

  • Analyse a set of sample phishing messages and identify warning signs.
  • Complete a guided lab worksheet and record defensive observations.

Weekly submission: Complete both practical tasks and submit files/screenshots/notes as instructed.

WEEK 04Risk assessment

Week 4 — Risk assessment

What you will learn

  • Identify assets, threats, likelihood and impact.
  • Use a basic risk matrix and prioritise remediation steps.

Practical assignments

  • Prepare a risk register for a fictional small organisation.
  • Create a secure configuration checklist for a sample system.

Weekly submission: Complete both practical tasks and submit files/screenshots/notes as instructed.

WEEK 05Defensive project

Week 5 — Defensive project

What you will learn

  • Review a fictional website/system profile and supplied evidence.
  • Map findings to practical defensive recommendations.

Practical assignments

  • Draft a security awareness and hardening plan.
  • Prepare a finding report with evidence, severity rationale and fixes.

Weekly submission: Complete both practical tasks and submit files/screenshots/notes as instructed.

WEEK 06Report and assessment

Week 6 — Report and assessment

What you will learn

  • Organise notes, scope, methodology, findings and limitations.
  • Explain responsible disclosure and why permission matters.

Practical assignments

  • Submit the cyber safety audit report and lab evidence.
  • Present findings and answer defensive security questions.

Weekly submission: Complete both practical tasks and submit files/screenshots/notes as instructed.

CAPSTONE • FINAL SUBMISSION

Your final project

A Cyber Safety Audit Report for a fictional small organisation or authorised training target, including scope, risk register, evidence notes and defensive recommendations.

Final deliverables: Project files, screenshots/demo evidence, short documentation or README, and an individual presentation/viva-style assessment.

CAREER PATHWAYS

What jobs can this skill lead towards?

This internship helps you build beginner-level exposure and a project to discuss in interviews. Job titles below are possible career directions—not a promise that this short internship alone qualifies you for each role.

Possible entry-level roles

  • SOC Analyst Trainee (requires further SIEM/log practice)
  • Cyber Security Intern
  • IT Support / Security Support Trainee
  • GRC / Security Documentation Assistant

Where to explore opportunities

Explore MSSPs, SOC service providers, IT support firms, consultancies and organisations with internal security teams. Many SOC roles require shift readiness, networking and SIEM skills.

What to learn next

  • Linux and networking deeper practice
  • SIEM tools, log analysis and incident response labs
  • Security certifications and more authorised lab experience

Job & internship alerts

Relevant security-awareness, IT support, SOC trainee and cyber-security internship notices may be shared when verified. Never test real systems without explicit written permission. No job placement is promised.

Important: Sharing an opportunity is not a referral, interview call or placement guarantee. Always apply through the employer's official channel and verify the listing.

ASSESSMENT & CERTIFICATE

How completion is evaluated

Assessment components

  • Practical task completion and quality.
  • Final project functionality, relevance and originality.
  • Documentation and submission discipline.
  • Ability to explain your work and answer questions.

Certificate eligibility

Certificate is assessment-based. Students who complete the required activities, submit the final project and pass the assessment may qualify, subject to the programme's final review. Application or attendance alone does not guarantee a certificate.

Career transparency: This is a learning internship, not a guaranteed placement programme. Employer names mentioned as examples are not claimed as partners. Job openings change frequently and students are responsible for checking current requirements.
